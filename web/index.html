📘 Automotive Suspension Physics & Dynamics Guide

1. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

When a vehicle passes over road bumps, the springs compress or extend. According to Hooke's Law, the spring produces a restoring force proportional to displacement:

F_spring = -k · x

Without damping, the car mass would oscillate endlessly between potential energy and kinetic energy at its natural frequency.

2. Viscous Damping & Shock Absorbers

Springs store mechanical energy, but shock absorbers (hydraulic dampers) dissipate it as heat. Viscous damping force is proportional to relative velocity:

F_damping = -c · (dx/dt)

Calibrated fluid orifices in the damper restrict flow, slowing oscillation cycles and maintaining tire-ground contact.

3. Governing Equation of Motion

m · x''(t) + c · x'(t) + k · x(t) = F(t)

Where m is effective quarter-car sprung mass (kg), c is damping coefficient (N·s/m), k is spring stiffness (N/m), and F(t) is the dynamic road excitation.

4. Natural Frequency & Damping Ratio

ω_n = √(k / m) [rad/s] f_n = ω_n / (2π) [Hz]
c_crit = 2 · √(m · k) [N·s/m] ζ = c / c_crit (Damping Ratio)

• Underdamped (ζ < 1.0): Oscillatory decay with damped frequency ω_d = ω_n√(1 - ζ²). Passenger cars aim for ζ ≈ 0.6 – 0.7.
• Critically Damped (ζ = 1.0): Fastest possible return to equilibrium with zero overshoot.
• Overdamped (ζ > 1.0): Sluggish, non-oscillatory return. Transmits harsh vertical shock to occupants.

5. Forced Oscillation & Resonance

f_road = v / λ [Hz] r = f_road / f_n (Frequency Ratio)

When vehicle forward speed v over road waves of length λ produces an excitation frequency matching the vehicle's natural frequency (r ≈ 1.0), RESONANCE occurs. Amplitude magnifies dramatically, which is why adequate damping is critical for vehicular safety!
